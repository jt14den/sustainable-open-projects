## Open Methods Kit

All names, capacities, and service profiles in this case are invented for teaching. They are not claims about real services, prices, or institutions.

Open Methods Kit publishes twelve openly licensed research-methods lessons and the source for its small website. Readers can reuse the public lessons without accounts. Educators send corrections by email because several do not use code-hosting tools. Two volunteers maintain the collection. Together they can sustain **two hours of maintenance each week** and **$25 per month** in service costs. The person who currently publishes updates will leave in six weeks. A second volunteer can edit text but has never published a change. The site works, but there is no documented rule for approving substantial changes or handling a maintainer's absence.

Decisions to practice: how to receive a correction; who approves a change that alters a lesson's meaning; how to publish reliably after the maintainer leaves. The case does not establish what all educators want. Learners must identify one question to ask them.

## Governance checkpoint

Anyone proposes. The editor asks a lesson user and a maintainer for input; their evidence can change the proposal. A designated content lead decides and records the reason in a public change note, without personal contact details. A trained backup follows the same rule during absence. If evidence is insufficient or disagreement remains, hold the substantive change and review it in one week; continue routine corrections. These are proposed roles, not extra staff already available in the case. The two volunteers must agree who can fill them and arrange training before relying on the backup.

## Infrastructure checkpoint

A is a provisional choice because it leaves some routine capacity for content work and fits the service budget. B saves the fee and offers institutional support but leaves no routine time margin. Test publication, recovery, and usable exports with the incoming maintainer before committing to A. Confirm continuing eligibility and the support scope before committing to B. Neither choice establishes that transition work fits the team's time allowance.

## Decision record starter

1. **Need and evidence:** Educators can submit corrections by email; we still need to ask ____.
2. **Risk, facets, and phase evidence:** The impending departure affects ____ because ____. A working website does not establish ____. Recheck this assessment when ____.
3. **Decision rule:** For a substantive change, ____ proposes, ____ is consulted, ____ decides, ____ records reasons, and ____ handles absence or disagreement.
4. **Infrastructure:** Choose ____ provisionally because ____. Alternative ____ has the advantage ____. Limitation: ____.
5. **Resources and continuity:** Recurring labor ____; recurring service cost ____; transition/training work ____; who can cover the handover ____ (or still unknown).
6. **Support and public status:** Accept ____; defer ____; review owner ____. Proposed maintenance status ____; release title/version/authors/date/reuse terms ____; preservation location or referral ____; who continues operation and support, if anyone ____. A preserved artifact is not a promise of service.
7. **Next action:** Uncertainty ____; action ____; responsible role ____; consult ____; review date or event ____; observation that would make us reconsider ____.

A justified handover, reduced scope, or responsible sunset can meet this task. Identify effects on users, consultation, and how the status will be communicated. Do not assume an institution has agreed to support the project.
