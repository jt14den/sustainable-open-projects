## Prepare for a pilot

This is a proposed 120-minute introductory workshop, not a measured schedule. ITAV is the organizing framework. Use the [reference crosswalk](../learners/reference.md) for the UC OSPO academic companion; do not teach a second vocabulary. The lesson's supplied case is enough. External links are optional, and draft playbook chapters are not teaching procedures.

Supply printed or saved learner episodes and the [project worksheet](../learners/project-worksheet.md). Check that the worksheet is reachable from the learner menu and all-in-one links. Offer plain text fields or labeled diagrams as equivalent formats. Keep the decision record visible from the start.

Episode metadata totals 35 teaching and 75 exercise minutes, plus the 10-minute break. Checks, reading, feedback, and transitions are inside these allocations. Alternate teaching and exercise segments in the order shown in each episode. Record actual times before claiming the schedule works. If time is short, cut extra whole-class reports and the optional model comparison before practice or feedback. Do not add the optional readings as required in-class work.

## Adjust support to demonstrated prior knowledge

Use the baseline and short individual responses, including “unsure,” to decide how much explanation is needed. The guided default gives a sound example before the major task, a partial starter, then a bounded choice. An experienced learner may make a short prediction first or skip the starter, but must explain it and meet the same criteria. Explicitly connect an initial attempt to the explanation and let the learner revise. Do not equate all struggle with harm or all failure with learning.

The earlier blank prompts asked learners to invent context, identify groups, choose a representation, and negotiate a decision at once. That is a design concern to investigate, not a measurement of cognitive load. Keep readable text and access options. The [teaching references](../learners/reference.md) support design choices with limits; the workshop has not tested them.

Start silently before pair discussion; rotate recorder and reviewer. Ask for contrasting examples rather than reports from every pair. Do not let a confident spokesperson be the only source of evidence. Personal disclosure is optional. Use worksheet checkpoints when a learner misses a task so later work remains possible.

## Observe and respond

| Observable sign | Response and feedback |
| --- | --- |
| “Everyone wants email/a forum” without evidence | Ask for the case sentence. Rewrite as a question and name someone to ask. |
| More contributors treated as free capacity | Ask who reviews, documents, and onboards, and what they will defer. |
| A governance label replaces an operational rule | Ask who decides a specific change tomorrow and where the reason goes. |
| Backup is only a name | Ask for a demonstrated task, access/recovery, authority, and available time. |
| Institutional support assumed | Ask which commitment is confirmed and who can authorize it. Use a referral task. |
| Maintenance and review each get the full time allowance | Put both in one weekly budget; allow reduced support or measured estimates. |
| “A DOI/archive keeps the service running” | Separate identifying a release, preserving files, and operating a service. Ask for an owner for each. |
| Only growth is accepted as success | Compare a justified reduced scope or sunset with an unfunded support promise. Include affected users and status communication. |

For an unsupported answer, explain the specific gap and let the learner revise one sentence. For a sound answer, change one condition rather than repeat all scaffolding. A reachable conduct-reporting route needs a responder with capacity and an alternative contact; a policy alone is not an operational response.

## Assess the proposed decisions

Use the worksheet's qualitative checklist, with no total score. Accept different governance arrangements and A/B choices when reasoning fits the evidence. Accept handover, reduced scope, or responsible sunset when the plan addresses users and separates preservation from operation. Unknown workload or institutional permission should be identified and checked, not invented. Completing the record is not stakeholder approval.

## Human pilot and evidence to collect

Recruit a small group resembling the two learner profiles, with a second instructor observing if possible. Before collecting or retaining example work, obtain permission. Use anonymous summaries where possible.

1. Record completion separately from quality: which fields and tasks were finished, and actual segment times.
2. Record where learners stop, ask what a prompt means, invent unsupported facts, or defer to the most experienced person. Note whether the starter and checkpoints helped.
3. Assess final records and individual exit explanations against the stated criteria. Keep permitted examples of adequate and incomplete reasoning. Record specific revisions needed, not just satisfaction.
4. Ask what was confusing and useful. Treat these answers as feedback, not direct evidence of learning.
5. Revise the largest observed obstacle first. If participants already reason fluently, consider a separate project clinic rather than adding beginner scaffolding.

Optional delayed task, offered with agreement one or two weeks later: first recall the decision questions without the handout; then reopen it and apply them to this changed constraint: **the monthly service budget becomes zero while time stays unchanged**. Name a provisional choice, a support boundary, and a check before commitment. B may fit the cash constraint, but its labor and eligibility still need attention; reduced scope or sunset can also be justified. Record unaided recall separately from supported application. This interval is a practical pilot choice, not a proven optimum.

Assess workplace use separately, for example whether someone consulted an affected user or tested a handover. Do not infer it from a good worksheet or positive feedback. Do not send or schedule follow-up messages without separate authorization.
