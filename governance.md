::: questions

- Who can approve a change, and how can input affect it?
- What evidence makes a backup workable?

:::

::: objectives

- Draft a consequential decision rule with consultation, authority, records, and continuity.
- Justify a tradeoff and identify a missing handover capability.

:::

## Make the rule operational

Governance is how a project makes and carries out decisions. A useful rule states who proposes, who is consulted, who decides, where reasons are recorded, and what happens during absence or disagreement.

Use the **Governance starter** in the [project worksheet](../learners/project-worksheet.md). This local activity is informed by ITAV. [Appendix A of the 2018 guidebook](https://itav.lyrasis.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ITAV_Report_Interactive.pdf#page=43), printed page 41, assesses sustainability across four facets. It is not a governance-model selection or scoring procedure.

:::: challenge

## Check the backup

**3 minutes, individual response and feedback.** The team names the second volunteer as backup publisher. What missing evidence would you seek before calling this a workable handover? Cite a case fact.

::: solution

The second volunteer has never published. Observe them publish a test correction, recover access, and explain their authority; also confirm time to take responsibility. Naming a person does not establish capability. One important missing condition with a reason is enough for this check. Revise a response that treats access alone as proof of readiness.

:::
::::

## Worked rule: a broken link

A reader proposes the replacement by email. The editor checks its destination. A maintainer approves and publishes it, recording the reason in a public change note. A trained backup uses the same procedure during absence. The proposed backup must first demonstrate the task and have time to act. These are roles for the existing team to agree, not newly available staff.

A substantial change can alter what educators teach, so a user should be consulted before approval. Consultation means their evidence can change or stop a proposal. If a conduct policy is offered, it also needs a reachable private reporting route, an available responder, and an alternative contact when the responder is involved. Do not promise an institutional response until that institution agrees.

## Complete and defend a decision rule

**14 minutes:** 2 silent drafting, 6 paired completion, 4 review and revision, 2 transition/checkpoint. Swap recorder and reviewer. Use your community table or its checkpoint, plus the governance starter row.

1. Complete the rule for a change that alters a lesson's meaning. State whose input can change it.
2. Set a fallback for absence and unresolved disagreement, including a review point.
3. Explain one tradeoff and one capability check before assigning the backup.
4. Review and revise using the criteria below. Keep the rule for your final record.

::: solution

The worksheet's **Governance checkpoint** is one acceptable rule: anyone proposes; a lesson user and maintainer advise; an agreed content lead decides and records reasons; a trained backup covers absence. Hold disputed substantive changes for review in one week while routine corrections continue. This sacrifices speed to avoid changing meaning without sufficient evidence.

Another acceptable rule lets both volunteers approve substantial changes together, with a pause when either is unavailable. It offers shared authority but risks delay; it needs a realistic review point and a plan for the departure.

**Criteria:** Explicit authority, meaningful consultation, recorded reasons, feasible roles, and absence/disagreement handling. A founder, foundation, or consensus label alone does not meet them. No committee or legal structure is required.

:::

## Model names describe different dimensions

Founder authority describes who has final say. Contribution-based influence describes how people gain responsibility; it should recognize work beyond code. Foundation support describes an organizational home. Consensus describes a decision procedure. These can coexist. Consensus requires a defined way to handle objections; voting requires a defined electorate and threshold. They are not synonyms. More detail is in the [reference](../learners/reference.md).

Cut the model comparison before cutting feedback.

::: keypoints

- Write rules for decisions, not just model labels.
- Consultation must be able to affect the decision.
- A backup needs authority, access, skill, and time.
