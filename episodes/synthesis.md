::: questions

- Do the proposed decisions fit together?
- What evidence would make us revise the proposal?

:::

::: objectives

- Produce a decision record connecting community, governance, technology, and resources.
- Distinguish citation, preservation, and ongoing support in a public status proposal.
- Revise a first action when the departure date changes.

:::

## Connect the decisions

Use **Decision record starter** and **Review checklist** in the [project worksheet](../learners/project-worksheet.md). You may use the checkpoint copies. Each learner keeps a record, even if a pair discusses the same choices.

A short status note must distinguish a citable release, a preserved artifact, and an operating service. For example: “Proposed status: corrections only during handover. Preserve the next numbered release with authors, date, reuse terms, and source files at a repository to be confirmed with the library. The incoming maintainer will test publication and recovery before accepting responsibility. A stored release does not promise ongoing support.” The location and commitment are unresolved, not completed deposits. The optional [playbook preservation chapter](https://github.com/UC-OSPO-Network/playbook/blob/563615951611866b1822b20a313091fa42d8077e/facet1/scholarly_preservation.md) offers academic context.

If nobody can maintain the service, a smaller offer or responsible sunset can be justified. Consult affected users, communicate an end or review date, preserve an identifiable release with its reuse terms, and say which functions will stop. A quiet repository alone does not establish failure.

:::: challenge

## Assemble, review, revise

**17 minutes:** 8 individual assembly, 5 peer review, 3 revision, 1 transition. Use the worksheet headings and all supplied outputs.

1. Connect the community need, risk and facets, rule, service choice, and resources. Include recurring and transition work, support limits, and one uncertain commitment.
2. Add a proposed public status/citation/preservation note. Name the release information, intended location or referral, and who would operate or support it, if anyone.
3. State a next action, responsible role, people to consult, and review date or event. Add an observation that would trigger reconsideration.
4. Have a partner apply every checklist row using **demonstrated / needs revision / not yet addressed**. Request one specific revision, then make it. Do not add a numerical total.

::: solution

A coherent proposal can retain emailed corrections, adopt the content-lead rule, and choose A provisionally while deferring new lessons. The editor's next action is a publishing and recovery rehearsal with the departing maintainer before handover. Measure review and documentation effort; do not assume the routine margin covers migration. Reconsider if the incoming maintainer cannot complete the task or total work exceeds capacity. Add the status note above with its unresolved repository referral.

B with narrower support, a negotiated institutional handover, or a planned sunset can also meet the criteria. Assess the evidence and effects on users, not whether the choice keeps the project growing. A record that promises support without staff time needs revision even if every heading is filled.

:::

:::: challenge

## Individual exit: departure tomorrow

**5 minutes, individual.** The publishing maintainer must leave tomorrow rather than at the case's planned date. Name the first action you would change, explain why, and identify another facet affected. Submit your own short response.

::: solution

Prioritize an access and recovery rehearsal with the incoming maintainer today, deferring new content. If that is not possible, communicate a temporary pause while preserving an identifiable release. This links technology capability to resources, governance authority, and community expectations. Other first actions pass when they respond to the changed deadline, cite a reason, and connect another facet. Merely renaming the backup does not address capability.

:::
::::

::: instructor

Use the 3 teaching minutes to connect the record and explain the status example. Review individual exit reasoning separately from record completion. A filled worksheet does not establish retention or workplace use. A learner with no authority can propose a consultation; never require a real institutional commitment to pass.

:::

## Optional transfer after the workshop

Apply the questions to your own project, or keep the fictional case. Name a decision, whom you must consult, and one limit to transferring this solution. No personal disclosure is required. An optional follow-up task appears in the instructor notes; it is not part of the 120-minute schedule.


::: keypoints

- Make a proposal that stakeholders can review and test.
- Citation, preservation, and continued operation require different commitments.
- A justified handover, smaller scope, or responsible sunset can meet the task.
- Use a changed condition to test reasoning; completion alone does not prove learning.

:::
