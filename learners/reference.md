## Working definitions

- **Sustainability:** keeping useful work and its responsibilities supportable as conditions change; this may include a planned ending.
- **Governance:** rules for decisions, participation, responsibility, and disagreement.
- **Stakeholder:** a person or group affected by a decision or able to affect it.
- **Resources:** money, staff or volunteer time, review, documentation, skills, and institutional commitments.
- **Technology:** the services and tools used to publish, receive contributions, and recover work.
- **Community Engagement:** learning from affected people and making their participation possible within stated support limits.
- **Citation:** information identifying and crediting a particular release.
- **Preservation:** retaining the artifact and information needed to interpret it; not a promise of continued operation.
- **Operation:** the ongoing work of keeping a service available and supported.

## ITAV source and adaptation

LYRASIS, [*It Takes a Village: Open Source Software Sustainability*](https://itav.lyrasis.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ITAV_Report_Interactive.pdf), February 2018, CC BY 4.0. The upload path has a later date. Printed pages 8–10 explain facets and phases; [Appendix A, printed page 41](https://itav.lyrasis.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ITAV_Report_Interactive.pdf#page=43) is a four-facet sustainability assessment, not a governance-model selection procedure.

This lesson uses that edition's phase labels: Getting Started; Growing/Getting Established; Stable, But Not Static. Assess each facet using evidence and uncertainty. Phases can differ across facets and change over time. The [later ITAV toolkit](https://itav.lyrasis.org/toolkit/) supplies further official activities and sometimes different wording. Do not silently mix editions.

The fictional case, operational rules, comparisons, and checklist are lesson adaptations informed by ITAV. They are not validated scores or official ITAV instruments. Extension beyond heritage software is a teaching choice. Official sources checked 25 November 2026.

## Governance names after the decision rule

| Name | Dimension and possible benefit | Question the name does not answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder authority (sometimes BDFL) | A clear final decision maker can act quickly | Who can challenge a decision or cover departure? |
| Consensus | A defined objection-resolution process can build agreement | What counts as consent and how does a deadlock end? |
| Contribution-based influence (sometimes meritocratic) | Responsibility can follow demonstrated work | Which contributions count, and who has access to contribute? |
| Foundation support | An organizational home may supply continuity | Which decisions belong to the foundation or project team? |

These dimensions can coexist. Voting specifies an electorate and threshold; consensus specifies treatment of objections. Either needs an explicit process, record, and fallback.

## Selected teaching evidence

- Greg Wilson, [Teaching Tech Together](https://teachtogether.tech/en/index.html): practical design and feedback guidance. It does not evaluate this workshop.
- Sinha and Kapur (2021), [When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105): some problem-first designs benefit learning under particular conditions. Use bounded attempts with consolidation, not unsupported discovery; direct transfer to this workshop is untested.
- Dieterich, Rumann, and Rodemer (2025), [Conditions for Effective Learning from Erroneous Examples](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10071-x): effects depend on prompts, explanations, and prior knowledge. Our repair prompt follows a sound example.
- Agarwal, Nunes, and Blunt (2021), [Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9): broader classroom evidence supports retrieval practice, with task and setting limits. A short adult workshop still needs its own retention and application checks.

These annotations and the 15-source research review supplied with this revision distinguish empirical findings from design guidance. No source establishes this lesson's effectiveness. Reference review date: 25 November 2026.
