::: questions

- Which service fits the proposed work and support boundary?
- What must be checked before relying on a provider?

:::

::: objectives

- Compare supplied options using case constraints and a credible alternative.
- Separate recurring work from transition effort and propose a continuity check.

:::

## Count the whole workflow

Infrastructure includes publishing, contribution channels, storage, and recovery. Open **Fictional service profiles** and **Infrastructure comparison** in the [project worksheet](../learners/project-worksheet.md). All features and prices are invented teaching inputs. No vendor research is needed.

Maintenance is only part of the work. Add review, responses, documentation, and one-off migration or handover. Institutional support is a commitment to confirm, not spare labor to assume. Funding and legal questions should become referrals to the appropriate local office, not decisions based on this workshop.

:::: challenge

## Check available capacity

**3 minutes, individual response and feedback.** Profile B uses the full routine maintenance allowance. Can it absorb new correction requests? Explain what the case does and does not establish.

::: solution

No spare review capacity is stated. Estimate or observe review effort before promising it. B might work with a reduced support commitment or confirmed reallocation of work. Neither “free service means free support” nor “B can never work” follows from the facts. Revise an unsupported claim to state the missing evidence.

:::
::::

## Worked comparison

Reject C under current staffing: its maintenance demand exceeds available labor, even though its service fee fits. Low cash cost does not supply missing time.

A leaves more room for review than B. A provisional plan could reserve that remaining time for corrections and documentation and defer new lessons. That is a planning assumption to test, not a measured workload. B saves money and offers institutional support; it may fit a narrower service if eligibility and responsibilities are confirmed.

Before relying on either, have the incoming maintainer demonstrate publication and recovery. Test whether an export can be understood and used elsewhere. Record who owns each dependency and what happens if access or institutional eligibility ends. The optional [playbook funding introduction](https://github.com/UC-OSPO-Network/playbook/blob/563615951611866b1822b20a313091fa42d8077e/facet2/funding_intro.md) provides academic context; it does not authorize campus commitments.

:::: challenge

## Choose, then repair a flawed decision

**14 minutes:** 2 silent comparison, 6 paired choice, 4 feedback and repair, 2 transition/checkpoint. Swap roles. Use the case, profiles, and your earlier outputs or their checkpoints.

1. Complete the A/B comparison. Select a provisional option using two case facts and name an advantage of the other option.
2. Explain how educators without publishing accounts can submit corrections, within your support boundary.
3. Identify recurring maintenance, review and documentation work, plus one-off handover work. State what is deferred or needs an institutional commitment. Unknown estimates must remain unknown.
4. Name a dependency, continuity uncertainty, and check before commitment.
5. Repair this flawed claim: “B costs nothing, so we can promise same-day replies to every correction.” Use a case fact and propose a defensible replacement.

::: solution

A fits the service budget and leaves a routine time margin. B saves the fee and offers support, but its maintenance consumes the available allowance. Either can pass if the proposal accounts for the whole workflow and unresolved commitments. Email corrections remain possible under both profiles.

For the flawed claim, replace the same-day promise with a limited review offer pending workload measurement, or pause support and communicate that limit. Confirm B's ongoing eligibility and support scope. For A, test publication, account recovery, and usable exports. Transition time remains unknown and must be budgeted before commitment. Use **Infrastructure checkpoint** if needed.

**Criteria:** Use case evidence, distinguish facts from estimates, include the other option's advantage, and address labor, money, access, and continuity. Popularity and price alone are insufficient.

:::
::::

::: instructor

Use 8 teaching minutes for the whole-work budget (3), rejecting C (2), and A/B reasoning (3). Pause if learners spend the time allowance twice, once on maintenance and again on review. Ask them to put both tasks in the same weekly budget. Accept reduced support or a planned sunset if effects on users and preservation are addressed.

:::

Next, combine the choices and state what would make you reconsider them.


::: keypoints

- Compare the whole workflow, not just service fees.
- Recurring work and one-off transition work both need capacity.
- Test handover and dependencies before making a commitment.

:::
