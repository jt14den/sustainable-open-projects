::: questions

- Which needs are established by the case?
- What support can this project actually offer?

:::

::: objectives

- Distinguish a case fact from an assumption about a group.
- Propose a way to test a community need and a feasible support boundary.

:::

## Identify people and evidence

Use **Community starter** in the [project worksheet](../learners/project-worksheet.md). A stakeholder is someone affected by a decision or able to affect it. A reader may also be a contributor. Corrections, documentation, testing instructions, translation, and review are contributions even when no code changes.

:::: challenge

## Check the claim

**3 minutes, individual response and feedback.** Several educators send corrections by email. Write one supported fact and one preference the case does not establish. Include a reason, or state what you are unsure about.

::: solution

Some educators use email now. We do not know whether all prefer email or whether they would use another route. Ask an affected educator to try draft instructions. Treating maintainer convenience as proof of user preference needs revision. Revise one unsupported sentence after feedback.

:::
::::

## Worked row: educators

The first worksheet row starts from observed behavior. A code-hosting account requirement might exclude some educators. Asking one educator to try a correction route tests a specific barrier, but cannot establish everyone's preference. Record that limit and seek other perspectives as capacity allows.

A proposed support boundary is: accept emailed corrections, reserve a limited review slot, and defer new lesson requests during handover. Review and response take time, so do not promise immediate answers. Publishing without ongoing support is also a legitimate choice if it is clearly communicated. See the optional [playbook community introduction](https://github.com/UC-OSPO-Network/playbook/blob/563615951611866b1822b20a313091fa42d8077e/facet3/community_intro.md); participation arrangements are choices, not a maturity ladder.

:::: challenge

## Complete the community table

**14 minutes:** 2 silent writing, 6 paired completion, 4 review and revision, 2 transition/checkpoint. Use the case and starter row. One person records; the other reviews evidence. Swap roles next episode.

1. Complete the reader and maintainer rows. A text-labeled diagram with the same evidence and questions is an equivalent route.
2. Label one fact and one assumption. Write a question for a named group that tests the assumption.
3. Choose one group whose exclusion would change the publishing decision; explain why.
4. Write one sentence saying what support and contributions the team will accept, what it will defer, and who must review incoming work. Do not assume extra volunteers exist.
5. Review against the criteria below and revise one sentence. Keep the table and boundary for your final record.

::: solution

A reader needs a public route to the lessons, but whether readers can locate corrected versions is unknown. Ask a reader to try. The incoming maintainer can edit but has not published; rehearse a change together. Excluding that maintainer could produce a workflow nobody can run after departure.

A bounded proposal is to accept emailed corrections for scheduled review by the editor, while pausing new lessons during handover. Its workload must still be measured. A smaller support offer can pass if it addresses affected users and communicates the limit.

**Criteria:** Cite facts, mark uncertainty, give a concrete participation route, and account for review work. A long stakeholder list alone is insufficient. Use **Community checkpoint** in the worksheet if you need to resume later.

:::
::::

::: instructor

Use 8 teaching minutes for stakeholder definitions (2), the worked row (3), and support boundaries (3). Pause if learners claim everyone wants a forum. Ask them to point to the supporting case sentence, then rewrite the claim as a question. If they propose recruiting people as free capacity, ask who onboards and reviews them. Never require personal project disclosure.

:::

Next, decide who can act on the input and how decisions are recorded.


::: keypoints

- A stated behavior is evidence, not proof of every user’s preferences.
- Recognize contributions beyond code and the work needed to review them.
- A clear, limited support offer can fit a project better than growth.

:::
